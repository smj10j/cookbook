pitch: >-
  Orzo cooked risotto-style is the trick here — you toast the little pasta grains,
  then simmer them in broth until they're creamy and tender, finished with lemon
  zest, olive oil, and a fistful of dill and parsley. Cherry tomatoes blister in
  a hot skillet until their skins split and go jammy, and the snapper sears in the
  same pan until the outside is golden and the inside just flakes. *Crumble the
  feta over while everything's hot so it softens at the edges and melts into the
  lemony orzo.* It's a Greek taverna plate that comes together in one pan and half
  an hour.

ingredients:
  - section: Lemon-herb orzo
    items:
      - ¾ cup orzo
      - 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
      - 1½ cups vegetable broth
      - Zest and juice of ½ lemon
      - 2 tbsp fresh dill, chopped
      - 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
  - section: Snapper & tomatoes
    items:
      - 2 red snapper fillets (about 6 oz each)
      - 1 tbsp olive oil
      - ½ tsp dried oregano
      - Salt and pepper
      - 1 cup cherry tomatoes
      - ⅓ cup feta, crumbled
      - 2 tbsp Kalamata olives, sliced
      - ½ lemon, cut into wedges
steps:
  - section: Orzo
    items:
      - Warm the olive oil in a skillet over medium heat and toast the orzo, stirring, until lightly golden, 2–3 minutes.
      - Add the broth, bring to a simmer, and cook, stirring often, until the orzo is tender and creamy and the liquid is mostly absorbed, 9–11 minutes. Stir in the lemon zest, juice, dill, and parsley. Scrape into a bowl and cover.
  - section: Tomatoes & fish
    items:
      - Wipe the skillet, add a little olive oil, and blister the cherry tomatoes over high heat until the skins split and char in spots, 3–4 minutes. Set aside.
      - Pat the snapper dry and season both sides with oregano, salt, and pepper. Add the tablespoon of oil to the hot skillet and sear the fillets 3–4 minutes on the first side until golden, then flip and cook 2–3 minutes more until they flake.
  - section: Assemble
    items:
      - Spoon the orzo onto plates and lay a fillet on top. Scatter the blistered tomatoes, Kalamata olives, and crumbled feta around, and serve with lemon wedges.
tips:
  - Toasting the orzo before it simmers gives it a nutty depth and keeps the grains separate instead of gluey.
  - Feta and olives are salty, so go light on salt in the orzo and on the fish — you can always add more at the table.
  - Pat the fillets very dry before searing; surface moisture steams the fish and prevents that golden crust.
  - Stir the orzo often as it simmers, like a risotto, to release its starch and turn it creamy.
extras:
  - label: Swap the fish
    note: "Any Mediterranean-friendly white fish slots right in. *Branzino, sea bass, cod, or halibut* are naturals — thicker fillets (cod, halibut) need a minute or two longer per side, or finish them in a 400°F oven. *Tilapia or sole* are thin and delicate: sear briefly, 2 minutes a side, and handle gently. *Salmon* works too — sear it skin-on for extra crispness and expect a richer plate against the lemony orzo. Keep the oregano seasoning and the orzo exactly the same; only the sear time shifts with thickness."
  - label: From the garden
    note: Use garden cherry tomatoes for blistering and swap the dried oregano for fresh, plus dill or parsley straight from the bed.
  - label: Make it heartier
    note: Add a handful of baby spinach or arugula to the orzo off the heat so it just wilts, or fold in a spoonful of the blistered tomatoes for extra brightness.

Cooking orzo like a risotto — toasted, then simmered in broth with plenty of
stirring — turns a bag of pasta into something creamy and restaurant-worthy. It's
the perfect bed for a simple pan-seared fillet.
