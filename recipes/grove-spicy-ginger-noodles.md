ingredients:
  - section: Ginger-garlic tamari sauce
    items:
      - 3 tbsp tamari
      - 1½ tbsp rice vinegar
      - 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
      - 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated
      - 2 cloves garlic, grated
      - 2 tsp maple syrup
      - 1 tsp chili crisp, plus more to taste
  - section: Noodles & vegetables
    items:
      - 6 oz wide rice noodles
      - 1 tbsp neutral oil
      - 4 oz shiitake or cremini mushrooms, sliced
      - 1 shallot, thinly sliced
      - 1½ cups broccoli florets
      - ½ red bell pepper, thinly sliced
      - 1 carrot, julienned
  - section: Crispy tofu (optional add-on)
    items:
      - 7 oz extra-firm tofu, pressed and cubed
      - 2 tsp cornstarch
      - 2 tsp neutral oil
  - section: To finish
    items:
      - 3 tbsp roasted peanuts, chopped
      - 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
      - 3 scallions, thinly sliced
      - Handful of cilantro leaves
steps:
  - section: Prep
    items:
      - If using tofu, press it 15 minutes, cube it, and toss with the cornstarch.
      - Whisk the sauce ingredients together. Soak or boil the noodles per the package, pulling them a minute early so they're pliable but still firm; rinse and drain.
  - section: Cook
    items:
      - Heat the cast iron (or a wok) over medium-high. If using tofu, crisp it in the 2 tsp oil, turning, 8–10 minutes until golden on all sides; set aside.
      - Add the 1 tbsp oil and the mushrooms in one layer; leave them alone 3 minutes to brown, then stir and add the shallot.
      - Add the broccoli and a splash of water, cover 2 minutes, then toss in the pepper and carrot for 1 minute — they should stay crisp.
      - Add the noodles and the sauce and toss over high heat 1–2 minutes, until the sauce is absorbed and the noodles are glossy.
  - section: Serve
    items:
      - Divide between bowls, top with the tofu, and finish with peanuts, sesame seeds, scallions, and cilantro. Pass more chili crisp at the table.
tips:
  - Rice noodles keep cooking in the pan — if they're fully soft before they hit the sauce, they'll break and go gummy.
  - Sear the mushrooms alone and don't stir them early; crowded or fussed-over mushrooms steam instead of browning.
  - Add the tofu at the very end so it stays crisp instead of soaking up the sauce.
  - The Grove's version is only lightly spicy. For more heat, double the chili crisp in the sauce or slice a fresh serrano over the top.
extras:

A copycat of the Grove's Spicy Ginger Noodles, rebuilt from the menu card and a lot of
tasting. The restaurant keeps it vegan and gluten-free, and so does this version —
tamari, not soy, and rice noodles.
