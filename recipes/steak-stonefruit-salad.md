  Peak-summer stone fruit is the secret ingredient here: nectarines get halved and laid

ingredients:
  - section: Steak
    items:
      - 10 oz sirloin or flat iron steak
      - 1 tsp neutral oil
      - Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  - section: Salad
    items:
      - 3 oz baby kale (or arugula or mixed greens)
      - 2 nectarines (or peaches or plums), halved and pitted
      - ¼ red onion, thinly sliced
      - ⅓ cup walnuts, toasted and coarsely chopped
  - section: Ginger-basil dressing
    items:
      - 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
      - 3 tbsp chopped fresh basil (Thai basil if you have it)
      - 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
      - 2 tbsp lime juice
      - 1 tbsp honey
      - Salt and freshly ground black pepper
steps:
  - section: Prep
    items:
      - Heat the grill to high (~500°F) and oil the grates. Pat the steak dry, rub with the neutral oil, and season generously with salt and pepper.
      - Whisk together the ginger, basil, olive oil, lime juice, honey, and a pinch of salt and pepper for the dressing. Set aside.
      - Toast the walnuts in a dry pan over medium heat until fragrant, 3–4 minutes; chop.
  - section: Grill
    items:
      - Grill the steak to a dark crust, 3–4 minutes per side, pulling it at 125°F for medium-rare. Rest 5–10 minutes, then slice thinly against the grain.
      - While the steak rests, lay the nectarine halves and red onion cut-side down on the grill and cook until lightly charred, 3–5 minutes. Cut the nectarines into ½-inch wedges.
  - section: Assemble
    items:
      - Toss the kale with about half the dressing and spread across two plates.
      - Scatter the grilled nectarines and onion over the greens, then fan the sliced steak on top.
      - Shower with toasted walnuts and drizzle with the remaining dressing.
tips:
  - Slice against the grain — find the direction of the muscle fibers and cut perpendicular for the most tender bites.
  - Grill the fruit only until grill marks form; go too long and it turns to mush. Firm-ripe (not soft) nectarines hold their shape best.
  - Pull the steak 5°F below your target and let carryover finish it during the rest so it stays pink against the cool greens.
  - No grill? Sear the steak in a hot cast-iron pan and caramelize the fruit cut-side down in the same skillet.
extras:
  - label: From the garden
    note: Garden basil is the whole dressing here — Thai basil adds an anise note, but sweet basil works beautifully too.
  - label: Swap the fruit
    note: Any ripe stone fruit shines — peaches, plums, pluots, or apricots. In a pinch, halved figs are gorgeous.
  - label: Pairs with
    note: A chilled light red — Gamay or a cool Pinot Noir — echoes the fruit without fighting the char.
