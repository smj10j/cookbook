pitch: >-
  This is Chicago deep dish built the real way, upside down. Cheese goes straight
  onto the dough, the fillings go over it, and a chunky, uncooked tomato sauce goes
  over everything. The crust is laminated like a quick pastry: roll the risen dough
  out, spread it with soft butter, roll it up tight, and give it a cold rest. Baked
  in a well-oiled cast iron pan, the edge fries until it's crisp and almost flaky.
  One half gets a lean homemade fennel-and-chili beef sausage pressed in raw as a
  single patty and topped with hot giardiniera. The other half gets balsamic-glazed mushrooms, slow-caramelized onions, squeezed-dry spinach, two kinds
  of fire-roasted peppers, and fresh chile from the garden.
  *The cheese layer is the trick: it seals the dough so neither half goes soggy,
  however much you pile on.*

ingredients:
  - section: Buttery cornmeal crust
    items:
      - 3¾ cups all-purpose flour
      - ⅓ cup fine yellow cornmeal
      - 2 tsp instant yeast
      - 2½ tsp sugar
      - 1 tsp kosher salt
      - 1¼ cups warm water (about 110°F)
      - 3 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
      - 5 tbsp unsalted butter, very soft (for laminating)
      - 2 tbsp olive oil (for the pan)
  - section: Cheese (whole pizza)
    items:
      - 20 oz low-moisture part-skim mozzarella, sliced
  - section: Beef sausage & giardiniera half
    items:
      - 12 oz lean ground beef (93% lean)
      - 1 tsp fennel seed, toasted and lightly crushed
      - 2 cloves garlic, grated
      - ½ tsp red pepper flakes
      - ½ tsp dried oregano
      - ½ tsp smoked paprika
      - ½ tsp kosher salt
      - ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
      - 1 tbsp cold water
      - ½ cup hot giardiniera, drained and chopped
  - section: Garden vegetable half
    items:
      - 1 red bell pepper
      - 1 yellow or orange bell pepper
      - 12 oz cremini mushrooms, sliced
      - 1 yellow onion, thinly sliced
      - 1½ tbsp olive oil
      - 2 cloves garlic, minced
      - 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 2 tsp chopped fresh rosemary)
      - 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar
      - 1 jalapeño or serrano, very thinly sliced
      - 5 oz baby spinach
      - Pinch of kosher salt
      - Handful fresh basil leaves, for finishing
  - section: Chunky sauce & finish
    items:
      - 28 oz no-salt-added crushed tomatoes (1 large can)
      - 2 cloves garlic, grated
      - 1 tsp dried oregano
      - 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
      - ½ tsp sugar
      - 1 tsp red wine vinegar
      - ⅓ cup grated Parmesan
steps:
  - section: Dough (start 3–4 hours ahead, or the day before)
    items:
      - Whisk the flour, cornmeal, yeast, sugar, and salt in a large bowl. Add the warm water and melted butter, mix into a shaggy dough, then knead 4–5 minutes until just smooth. Stop there; *a short knead keeps the crust tender, not bready.* This is a slightly smaller batch than most deep-dish doughs, so the crust bakes thinner and crisper.
      - Put the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and let it rise in a warm spot until doubled, 1–1½ hours.
      - Laminate it. Punch the dough down and roll it into a rough 14 × 11-inch rectangle. Spread the soft butter over it edge to edge. Roll it up tightly from a long side like a jelly roll, flatten the log, fold it in thirds, and shape it into a ball.
      - Cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour (or up to overnight). The cold rest firms the butter into thin layers, and those layers are what make the crust flaky.
  - section: Fillings (while the dough rises)
    items:
      - Make the sausage. Toast the fennel seed in a dry pan until fragrant, then crush it. Work it into the beef with the garlic, pepper flakes, oregano, paprika, salt, pepper, and cold water, using your hands, until the mixture turns slightly tacky. The water keeps lean beef juicy in the oven. Refrigerate it raw.
      - Char both bell peppers over a gas burner, on the grill, or under the broiler until they're black all over. Steam them in a covered bowl for 10 minutes, then peel, seed, and slice them into strips.
      - Caramelize the onion. Warm ½ tbsp of the olive oil in a cast iron pan over medium-low, add the onion, and cook, stirring every few minutes, until it's soft, sticky, and deep amber, 20–25 minutes. Add a splash of water if it starts to catch. Scrape it onto a plate. *Slow-cooked onion brings a sweet, savory depth that does the job salt usually does.*
      - Turn the heat to medium-high and add the remaining 1 tbsp oil. Add the mushrooms in one layer (in two batches if they crowd the pan) and leave them alone until they're deeply browned and their liquid has cooked off. Add the garlic and thyme for 30 seconds, then pour in the balsamic and stir until it cooks down to a glaze that coats the mushrooms, about 1 minute. Season with a pinch of salt and add them to the onions.
      - Wilt the spinach in the same pan, let it cool, then wring it bone-dry in a clean towel. Wet vegetables are what make deep dish soggy.
      - Make the sauce. Drain the crushed tomatoes in a fine sieve for 15 minutes, then stir in the garlic, oregano, olive oil, sugar, and vinegar. Don't salt it; the Parmesan seasons it, and the vinegar brightens it without adding sodium. Don't cook it either; it reduces in the oven.
  - section: Build & bake
    items:
      - Put a rack in the lowest position and heat the oven to 425°F. Coat the 14-inch skillet with the olive oil, bottom and sides.
      - Roll the cold dough into a thin round about 17 inches across. Drape it into the pan and press it into the corners and about 1½ inches up the sides. Trim any thick overhang.
      - Shingle the mozzarella over the entire bottom of the crust. Run the slices right up to the walls so they seal the dough.
      - On the sausage half, press the raw sausage into one thin, even patty that covers the cheese edge to edge, then scatter the giardiniera over it. On the vegetable half, layer the spinach, the mushrooms and onions, and the roasted peppers, then scatter the sliced chile over the top.
      - Spoon the sauce over the whole pizza all the way to the crust walls, then shower it with Parmesan.
      - Bake 28–35 minutes, until the crust walls are deep golden and the sauce is bubbling at the edges. The sausage center should read 160°F. If the Parmesan browns too fast, tent the top loosely with foil.
      - Let the pizza rest in the pan for 10 minutes so the cheese sets. Run a thin spatula around the edge and slide it onto a board, or cut it in the pan. Tear basil over the vegetable half and cut it into wedges.
tips:
  - Short on time? Buy a 1-lb ball of fresh pizza dough from the bakery or deli case (Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, and most supermarket bakeries sell them) and use 1½ balls for the 14-inch pan. Laminate it with the soft butter the same way and chill it for 30 minutes. You lose the cornmeal crunch but keep most of the flakiness.
  - After baking, the sauce hides which half is which. Before it goes in, line up a few strips of red pepper along the seam on the vegetable side, or set the skillet handle at the meat side, so you can cut cleanly between the halves.
  - If the bottom crust is pale when you slide it out, slide it back into the skillet and set it over medium heat on the stovetop for 2–3 minutes to crisp the underside.
  - If you use Diamond Crystal kosher salt instead of Morton, the salt amounts here contain about 40% less sodium, because its flakes are lighter. Either brand works; Diamond just tastes milder at the same measure.
extras:
  - label: 10-inch pan (serves 3)
    note: A 10-inch skillet is almost exactly half the area of a 14-inch, so halve everything. Use 1¾ cups plus 2 tbsp flour, 2½ tbsp cornmeal, 1 tsp yeast, 1¼ tsp sugar, ½ tsp salt, ½ cup plus 2 tbsp water, 1½ tbsp melted butter, 2½ tbsp laminating butter, and 1 tbsp oil for the pan. For the fillings, use 6 oz beef with half the spices, 10 oz mozzarella, half of each vegetable, and a 14.5-oz can of no-salt-added crushed tomatoes. Roll the dough to about 13 inches and bake 25–30 minutes.
  - label: From the garden
    note: In tomato season, swap the can for about 2 lb garden tomatoes, peeled, chopped, and drained well. Tear plenty of garden basil over the vegetable half, use your own thyme or rosemary for the mushrooms, and pick the jalapeño or serrano straight from the garden. Make your own giardiniera with sliced serrano or jalapeño, cauliflower, carrot, and celery pickled in vinegar and olive oil for a few days.
  - label: Make ahead
    note: The laminated dough keeps in the fridge overnight and gets more flavorful. The sausage mix, roasted peppers, caramelized onions, glazed mushrooms, and squeezed spinach all keep a day ahead, so dinner is just assembly and baking.
  - label: Pairs with
    note: A Chianti or Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, or a cold lager. Serve a lemony arugula salad from the garden on the side to cut the richness.

Two pizzas in one skillet: a Chicago-style beef sausage half for tradition and a
garden vegetable half piled with peppers, glazed mushrooms, and caramelized onions. The butter-laminated cornmeal crust holds
up under both. It's a weekend project, but the dough and fillings can all be made the
day before.
