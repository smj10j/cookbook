  This is panzanella that eats like a salad, not a fillet parked on tomatoes.
  You salt ripe heirlooms and let them weep, catch that rosy juice for the

  bread. *The move is the crispy skin — crumble it back over the top like
  savory bacon bits and lemon zest wakes the whole thing up.* Ten minutes to
  rest and the bread goes silky at the center, crunchy at the edges.

ingredients:
  - section: Salmon
    items:
      - 2 salmon fillets (about 6 oz each), skin-on
      - Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
      - 1 tbsp olive oil, for searing
  - section: Salad
    items:
      - 5 oz day-old sourdough or ciabatta, torn into 1-inch pieces
      - 2 tbsp olive oil, for toasting the bread
      - 1 lb ripe tomatoes (heirloom and cherry), cut into big chunks
      - ½ tsp kosher salt, for the tomatoes
      - 3 cups baby arugula
      - ½ large cucumber, seeded and cut into half-moons
      - ¼ small red onion, thinly sliced
      - ¼ cup torn fresh basil leaves
      - 1 tbsp capers, drained (optional)
  - section: Lemon tomato-water dressing
    items:
      - 3 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
      - 1½ tbsp red wine vinegar
      - 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
      - 1 tsp lemon zest
      - 1 clove garlic, minced or grated
      - 1 tsp Dijon mustard
      - Pinch of salt and freshly ground black pepper
steps:
  - section: Salt the tomatoes
    items:
      - Cut the tomatoes into big chunks, toss with the ½ tsp salt in a colander set over a bowl, and let them drain 15–20 minutes. Save every drop of the juice that collects — that's the base of the dressing.
  - section: Toast the bread
    items:
      - Toss the torn bread with 2 tbsp olive oil and a pinch of salt. Toast in a dry skillet over medium heat (or on a sheet pan at 400°F) 8–10 minutes, tossing, until deep gold and crisp at the edges but still chewy inside. Set aside — this is the salad's savory crunch.
  - section: Sear the salmon, then flake it
    items:
      - Pat the fillets bone-dry and season both sides with salt and pepper. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a cast-iron skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the salmon in skin-side down and press gently for 20 seconds so it stays flat.
      - Sear undisturbed 4–5 minutes until the skin is crackly and releases on its own, then flip and cook 1–2 minutes more to 125–130°F. Rest on a plate 5 minutes.
      - Peel the shattered skin off in one piece and set it aside. Break the flesh into big craggy flakes with two forks, discarding any pin bones. Snap the crispy skin into shards for the top.
  - section: Dress, toss & finish
    items:
      - Whisk the reserved tomato juice with the vinegar, lemon juice, zest, garlic, Dijon, remaining olive oil, salt, and pepper until emulsified.
      - In a big bowl, combine the toasted bread, drained tomatoes, cucumber, red onion, capers, arugula, and most of the basil. Pour over the dressing, add the flaked salmon, and toss gently so the fish, greens, and bread mingle without breaking the salmon to mush. Let it sit 10 minutes so the bread drinks the juices.
      - Pile into two shallow bowls and shower with the crumbled crispy skin, the last of the basil, and a grind of pepper. Serve with a lemon wedge.
tips:
  - Day-old bread is non-negotiable — fresh bread turns to mush. If yours is fresh, tear it and dry it in a 300°F oven for 10 minutes first.
  - Salt the tomatoes and hoard the juice; that rosy tomato water plus a hit of lemon is the bright, summery soul of the dressing.
  - For shattering skin, get the fillet bone-dry, use a hot pan, and don't move it until it releases on its own — chasing it early is how the skin tears. Peeling it off to crumble on top keeps it crackly instead of steaming soft under the fish.
  - Add the arugula and toss just before serving so it stays perky, and eat within 30 minutes — you want the bread juicy with a little chew, not a soggy sponge.
extras:

  - label: Pairs with

Panzanella earns its keep on exactly the kind of night when the tomatoes are too
good to cook and the bread is a day past its best. Here it grows up into a real
salad: peppery arugula, flakes of crispy salmon folded right through the juicy
bread, and the shattered skin crumbled on top like savory bacon bits.
