  the brakes on all that heat — the plate swings from fiery to soothing and back.*

ingredients:
  - section: Jerk snapper
    items:
      - 2 red snapper fillets (about 6 oz each)
      - 2 scallions, roughly chopped
      - 2 cloves garlic
      - 1 tbsp fresh thyme (or 1 tsp dried)
      - 1 tsp ground allspice
      - ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
      - ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
      - ½ tsp cayenne (or 1 habanero, to taste)
      - 1 tbsp brown sugar
      - 1 tbsp soy sauce
      - 1 tbsp olive oil
      - Juice of 1 lime
  - section: Coconut rice & beans
    items:
      - ¾ cup jasmine rice, rinsed
      - ¾ cup light coconut milk
      - ¼ cup water
      - 1 cup black beans, drained and rinsed
      - 2 scallions, sliced
      - ¼ tsp salt
  - section: Avocado-cucumber salad
    items:
      - 1 avocado, diced
      - ½ cucumber, diced
      - 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped
      - Juice of ½ lime
      - 1 pinch salt
steps:
  - section: Marinate
    items:
      - Blend the scallions, garlic, thyme, allspice, cinnamon, nutmeg, cayenne, brown sugar, soy sauce, olive oil, and lime juice into a rough paste (a small blender or a good mince by hand both work).
      - Coat the snapper all over and let it marinate 20–30 minutes while you cook the rice.
  - section: Coconut rice & beans
    items:
      - Combine the rinsed rice, light coconut milk, water, and salt in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, cover, drop to low, and cook 15 minutes until the liquid is absorbed.
      - Off the heat, fold in the black beans and scallions, re-cover, and let steam 5 minutes. Fluff with a fork.
  - section: Sear & assemble
    items:
      - Heat a cast-iron or heavy skillet over medium-high with a film of oil. Sear the fillets 3–4 minutes per side until the crust is dark and the fish flakes — don't move them until they release.
      - Toss the avocado, cucumber, cilantro, lime, and salt. Serve the snapper over the coconut rice and beans with the salad alongside and extra lime.
tips:
  - Marinate at least 20 minutes but no more than an hour — the lime juice will start to "cook" the fish if it sits much longer.
  - Wait for the fish to release on its own before flipping; forcing it tears the caramelized jerk crust off the fillet.
  - Rinse the jasmine rice until the water runs clear so the coconut rice comes out fluffy, not gummy.
  - Control the heat with the cayenne or habanero — a whole habanero is authentically fiery; scale back for a milder plate.
extras:
  - label: Swap the fish
    note: "Jerk loves a firm, meaty fish that stands up to the hard sear. *Mahi-mahi, grouper, or snapper* are the classics; *cod or halibut* work well if you pat them very dry first so the crust forms. Thicker fillets (over ¾ inch) need an extra minute per side or a few minutes in a 400°F oven to finish. *Salmon* takes jerk beautifully — sear it skin-on and expect richer results. Even boneless chicken thighs work if you want to go landward — marinate longer (1–2 hours) and cook through to 165°F. Keep the marinade the same for all of them."
  - label: From the garden
    note: A garden habanero or scotch-bonnet in the marinade is the real-deal jerk heat, and garden thyme is central to the paste — use both if you've got them.

    note: This is finfish, so it's already Stephen-friendly — but if you ever build the coconut rice as a base for shrimp instead, keep a couple of snapper or mahi fillets going alongside so both of you are covered.

Jerk is as much about the aromatics — allspice (Jamaicans call it pimento),
thyme, and scallion — as it is about the heat. Cooking the rice in coconut milk
is the cooling counterweight that makes the whole plate sing.
