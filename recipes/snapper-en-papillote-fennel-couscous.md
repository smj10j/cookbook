ingredients:
  - section: Packets
    items:
      - 2 red snapper fillets (about 6 oz each), skin removed
      - 1 small fennel bulb, cored and shaved thin
      - 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
      - 2 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
      - 3 tbsp dry white wine
      - 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
      - 1 lemon (half sliced thin, half juiced)
      - 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
      - Salt and pepper
  - section: Lemon couscous
    items:
      - ¾ cup couscous
      - ¾ cup vegetable broth
      - 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
      - Zest of ½ lemon
      - 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
steps:
  - section: Prep
    items:
      - Heat the oven to 400°F. Cut two large squares of parchment (about 15 inches each).
      - Toss the shaved fennel, tomatoes, and garlic with a pinch of salt.
  - section: Build the packets
    items:
      - Mound half the fennel mixture on one half of each parchment square. Lay a fillet on top and season with salt and pepper.
      - Drizzle each with olive oil, white wine, and a squeeze of lemon juice, then top with a few thin lemon slices.
      - Fold the parchment over and crimp the edges in tight, overlapping pleats to seal a closed packet. Set on a sheet pan.
      - Bake 15–18 minutes — the packets will puff and brown. The fish is done when it flakes at the thickest point (open one to check).
  - section: Couscous & serve
    items:
      - While the fish bakes, bring the broth to a boil, stir in the couscous, cover, and pull off the heat. Let it sit 5 minutes, then fluff with the olive oil, lemon zest, and parsley.
      - Spoon the couscous into bowls. Slide each packet open (watch the steam), tip the fish and its broth over the couscous, and shower with parsley.
tips:
  - Slice the fennel paper-thin (a mandoline is ideal) so it softens in the same time the fish cooks — thick wedges stay crunchy.
  - Crimp the parchment edges tightly; a loose seal lets the steam escape and the fish dries out instead of poaching.
  - No parchment? Aluminum foil packets work the same way — just fold the edges over twice to lock in the steam.
  - Let the packet rest a minute before opening so the broth settles and the steam doesn't scald you.
extras:
  - label: Swap the fish
    note: "Almost any flaky white fish loves this method. *Cod, halibut, or sea bass* (thicker, ¾–1 inch) need 18–22 minutes — check for flake. *Tilapia, sole, or flounder* (thin) cook fast, 10–12 minutes, so pull them early. *Salmon* works beautifully too but is richer — skip the added olive oil in the packet and add a sprig of dill. For all of them, keep the fennel-tomato-lemon base as-is; just match the bake time to the thickness."

En papillote sounds fancy but it's the most forgiving way to cook fish — the
sealed parchment steams it gently and there's nothing to scrub afterward. Build
the packets ahead and bake them straight from the fridge when you're ready.
