title: Burmese Coconut Curry with Chicken & Udon Noodles

pitch: >-
  Think of this as Burma's answer to a bowl of spaghetti — ohn no khauk swè, the
  coconut-chicken noodle classic, streamlined for a weeknight. Chicken thighs get a
  quick dredge of chickpea flour and paprika so they brown into savory little nuggets
  and, better yet, thicken the broth to a velvety pour. From there it's a fragrant
  simmer of coconut milk, ginger, garlic, and turmeric loosened with a splash of
  stock and seasoned with fish sauce and tamari, wilted with spinach at the end.
  *Ladle it over thick udon and pile on the fixings — halved soft egg, raw onion,
  cilantro, and a hard squeeze of lime — so every bowl gets built to taste.*

ingredients:
  - section: Chicken
    items:
      - 10 oz boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1-inch pieces
      - 2 tbsp chickpea flour
      - 1 tsp paprika
      - Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  - section: Coconut curry
    items:
      - 2 tbsp neutral oil
      - 1 yellow onion, half thinly sliced, half finely chopped
      - 3 cloves garlic, minced
      - 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
      - 1 tsp ground turmeric
      - 1 (13.5-oz) can coconut milk
      - 1 cup chicken broth
      - 1 tbsp fish sauce
      - 1 tbsp tamari
      - 2 cups baby spinach
  - section: Noodles & garnish
    items:
      - 8 oz cooked udon noodles
      - 2 eggs, soft- or hard-boiled and halved
      - ¼ cup cilantro leaves
      - 1 lime, cut into wedges
steps:
  - section: Brown the chicken
    items:
      - Pat the chicken dry, season with salt and pepper, then sprinkle with the chickpea flour and paprika and toss to coat.
      - Warm the neutral oil in a wok or large frying pan over medium-high heat. Add the chicken and cook, stirring occasionally, until browned but not fully cooked through, 2–3 minutes. Transfer to a plate.
  - section: Build the curry
    items:
      - Add the chopped onion to the pan and cook until it begins to soften, 2–3 minutes. Stir in the garlic, ginger, and turmeric and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
      - Pour in the coconut milk and chicken broth, scraping up the browned bits, and bring to a simmer. Return the chicken and simmer until cooked through and the broth thickens slightly, 5–6 minutes.
      - Stir in the fish sauce and tamari, then the spinach, cooking just until wilted. Taste and adjust with more fish sauce or a squeeze of lime.
  - section: Assemble the bowls
    items:
      - Warm the udon (a quick dip in hot water or a minute in the simmering broth) and divide between two bowls.
      - Ladle the coconut chicken curry over the noodles. Top with the halved eggs, the reserved sliced raw onion, and cilantro.
      - Serve with lime wedges for squeezing at the table.
tips:
  - The chickpea-flour dredge does double duty — it crisps the chicken and thickens the broth, so don't skip it (it's also what keeps this gluten-friendly if you use tamari).
  - For a jammy 7-minute egg, lower eggs into boiling water, cook 7 minutes, then shock in ice water before peeling.
  - Toast the turmeric with the aromatics for a full minute — blooming it in the oil deepens the color and rounds out the raw edge.
  - Like it hotter? A spoonful of chili crisp or a pinch of gochugaru stirred in at the end wakes the whole bowl up.
extras:
  - label: From the garden
    note: A handful of garden cilantro and a few thin serrano rings on top nod to Burmese street-stall garnishes.
  - label: Swap the noodles
    note: Fresh or dried udon both work; rice noodles or even spaghetti (true to the ohn no khauk swè spirit) are fair game.
  - label: Make it vegetarian
    note: Swap the chicken for cubed firm tofu (dredge the same way) and the fish sauce for extra tamari plus a splash of lime.
