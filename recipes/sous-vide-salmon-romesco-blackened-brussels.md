ingredients:
  - section: Salmon & crackling
    items:
      - 2 salmon fillets (about 5½ oz each), skin-on, center-cut
      - 1 tsp kosher salt
      - ½ tsp sugar
      - 2 strips lemon zest
      - 2 sprigs garden thyme
      - 2 tbsp olive oil
      - ½ tsp neutral oil
      - Flaky salt, to finish
  - section: Smoky romesco
    items:
      - 1 red bell pepper
      - ½ cup garden cherry tomatoes (frozen is fine)
      - 3 tbsp almonds
      - ½ oz sourdough (about half a slice), torn
      - 1 clove garlic
      - 1½ tsp sherry vinegar
      - ½ tsp smoked paprika (pimentón)
      - 3 tbsp olive oil
      - 2 tsp olive oil
      - ¼ tsp kosher salt
      - A sliver of habanero (optional)
  - section: Blackened Brussels sprouts
    items:
      - 12 oz Brussels sprouts
      - 2 tbsp avocado oil
      - 1 tbsp butter
      - 1 clove garlic, thinly sliced
      - 1 serrano, thinly sliced
      - ½ lemon
      - Pinch of kosher salt
steps:
  - section: Prep
    items:
      - Heat the water bath to 122°F for silky, just-set salmon (125°F if you like it firmer). Heat the grill to high and the oven to 400°F.
      - Skin the fillets and scrape any gray fat off the inside of the skin with the back of the knife. Mix the salt and sugar, sprinkle it over the flesh, and refrigerate uncovered for 30 minutes.
      - If the tomatoes are frozen, dunk them in warm water until the skins slip off, then drain well.
  - section: Romesco & crackling
    items:
      - Grill the pepper over the hottest part of the fire, turning every few minutes, until blackened all over, 10–12 minutes. Cover it in a bowl to steam for 10 minutes, then peel and seed it — don't rinse away the smoke.
      - Toss the tomatoes with 1 tsp of the olive oil and roast until collapsed and jammy, 15–20 minutes. For the last 6–8 minutes, add the almonds and the bread tossed with the remaining 1 tsp oil.
      - Pat the salmon skin dry, rub it with the neutral oil and a pinch of salt, and press it flat between parchment under a second sheet pan. Roast 15–20 minutes, until rigid and glassy (give it 5 more minutes if it's still chewy), then cool on a rack.
      - Pulse the almonds, bread, and garlic to coarse sand. Add the pepper, tomatoes, paprika, vinegar, and salt, then stream in the 3 tbsp olive oil. Stop while it still looks like a coarse pesto and season to taste, adding the habanero if you want a slow burn.
  - section: Salmon
    items:
      - Rinse off the brine, pat the fillets dry, and bag each one with 1 tbsp olive oil, a strip of zest, and a thyme sprig. Seal gently — full suction crushes salmon.
      - Cook 40–45 minutes for a 1-inch fillet (50–55 for 1½ inches). It holds for another 20 minutes without changing, which is your buffer for the sprouts.
  - section: Sprouts & plate
    items:
      - Halve the sprouts through the core and save any loose leaves. Heat the cast iron over medium-high for 5 minutes, add the avocado oil, and lay the sprouts cut-side down. Salt them and leave them alone 4–5 minutes, until blackened at the edges, then toss and cook 2–3 minutes more.
      - Lower the heat to medium, add the butter, garlic, serrano, and loose leaves, and toss until the butter browns and the leaves crisp, about 1 minute. Squeeze in the lemon.
      - Swoosh the romesco across warm plates, lift the salmon on with a fish spatula, and finish it with flaky salt. Pile the sprouts alongside and stand a shard of crackling in each fillet.
tips:
  - The dry brine is what keeps sous-vide salmon from going mushy — it seasons the fish through and firms the surface so no white albumin weeps out.
  - Use lemon *zest* in the bag, not juice; over 45 minutes, sealed-in acid cures the surface and turns it chalky.
  - Frozen garden tomatoes make great romesco because the skins slip right off — just roast them down until jammy, or the sauce turns thin and pale.
  - 122°F doesn't pasteurize the fish, so treat it like sushi-grade salmon — buy the freshest you can and keep it in the bath under 2 hours.
extras:
  - label: From the garden
    note: Garden cherry tomatoes in the romesco, thyme in the bag and over the sprouts, serrano in the brown butter, and a sliver of habanero if you like heat.
  - label: Weeknight version
    note: Skip the bath and the crackling. Dry-brine the fillets skin-on, sear them skin-side down in a hot skillet for 4–5 minutes, flip, baste with the thyme and zest, and pull them at 120–125°F.
  - label: Make ahead
    note: Double the romesco — it keeps 5 days and is better on day two (roast the garlic if you make it hours ahead), and leftovers are great over eggs or grilled chicken. The crackling keeps a day in an airtight tin.
  - label: Gluten-free
    note: Swap the sourdough for an extra 2 tbsp of almonds; pulse in a few more if the sauce needs body.
