title: Sous-Vide Salmon with Smoky Romesco, Salmon-Skin Crackling & Blackened Brussels Sprouts

pitch: >-
  Peppers go straight onto a screaming grill until the skins are black and blistered all
  over, then get pounded with toasted almonds, oil-crisped sourdough and pimentón into a
  rustic, smoky romesco. The salmon is skinned and dry-brined, then slips into a 122°F bath
  until it's translucent coral and barely holding a flake. The skin goes into the oven
  pressed flat between two sheet pans, so it comes out as one glassy sheet you snap into
  shards. *Don't sear the bath-cooked fish: the crunch comes from the crackling standing
  in it.* Cast-iron Brussels sprouts, blackened cut-side down and finished with brown
  butter, serrano and lemon, give the plate its bitter char. Want a weeknight version?
  Pan-sear the fillets skin-on instead of using the bath.

ingredients:
  - section: Salmon
    items:
      - 2 salmon fillets (about 5½ oz each), skin-on, center-cut, 1–1¼ in thick
      - 1 tsp kosher salt (dry brine)
      - ½ tsp sugar (dry brine)
      - 2 strips lemon zest (peeled, no white pith)
      - 2 sprigs garden thyme
      - 2 tbsp olive oil
      - Flaky salt, to finish
  - section: Salmon-skin crackling (sous-vide path)
    items:
      - Skin from both fillets
      - ½ tsp neutral oil
      - Pinch of kosher salt
  - section: Romesco (about ½ cup — double it for leftovers)
    items:
      - 1 red bell pepper
      - ½ cup frozen garden cherry tomatoes
      - 3 tbsp almonds (skin-on is fine)
      - ½ oz sourdough bread (½ thick slice, crust on, torn)
      - 1 clove garlic, peeled
      - 1½ tsp sherry vinegar (or red wine vinegar)
      - ½ tsp smoked paprika (pimentón)
      - 3 tbsp olive oil
      - 2 tsp olive oil (for the bread and tomatoes)
      - ¼ tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
      - A sliver of habanero (optional)
  - section: Blackened Brussels sprouts
    items:
      - 12 oz Brussels sprouts
      - 2 tbsp avocado oil (or grapeseed)
      - 1 tbsp butter
      - 1 clove garlic, thinly sliced
      - 1 serrano, thinly sliced into rings
      - ½ lemon
      - Pinch of kosher salt
      - Thyme leaves, to finish
steps:
  - section: Setup (T–90)
    items:
      - Heat the water bath to *122°F / 50°C* for translucent, silky, just-set salmon (125°F / 52°C if you like it a little firmer and more opaque). Heat the grill to high and the oven to 400°F. Taking the pan-seared route? Skip the bath and **leave the skin on** the fillets.
      - "Skin the salmon (sous-vide path only). Lay the fillet skin-side down with the tail toward you, cut a small tab between flesh and skin, grip the skin with a paper towel, and push the knife away from you, blade angled slightly *down*, while you wiggle the skin side to side. Then scrape any grey fat off the inside of the skin with the back of the knife. That fat is what makes crackling chewy."
      - Mix the salt and sugar, sprinkle it evenly over the flesh side of the fillets, and refrigerate uncovered on a rack for 30–40 minutes.
      - Tip the frozen cherry tomatoes into a bowl of warm water for a minute or two. The skins split and slip right off. Pinch them off, then drain the tomatoes in a strainer so the extra water runs away.
  - section: Fire & oven (T–80 → T–60)
    items:
      - Set the pepper directly over the hottest part of the grill and turn it every 2–3 minutes until *fully blackened and blistered*, 10–12 minutes. (No grill? Use a gas burner or the broiler.) Move it to a bowl, cover tightly with a plate, and steam 10 minutes to loosen the skin.
      - Toss the drained tomatoes with 1 tsp of the olive oil and spread them on a small sheet pan. Roast at 400°F for 15–20 minutes, until they've collapsed, the juices have cooked down, and the edges are caramelized.
      - For the crackling, pat the skin very dry, rub it with the neutral oil and salt it lightly. Lay it scaly-side down on parchment on a sheet pan, cover with a second sheet of parchment, and press it under another sheet pan or a small skillet. Roast 15–20 minutes, checking at 12, until rigid and glassy. Cool it on a rack, where it keeps crisping.
      - Toss the torn sourdough with the remaining 1 tsp olive oil and roast it with the almonds on the tomato tray (or a separate one) for 6–8 minutes, checking at 5, until the almonds are golden inside (snap one to check) and the bread is crunchy.
  - section: Romesco (T–60)
    items:
      - Peel, stem and seed the pepper. **Don't rinse them**, because that washes away the smoke, and a few black flecks are good.
      - Pulse the almonds, bread and raw garlic in a mini food processor (a full-size bowl is too big for a half batch) until they look like coarse sand. Add the pepper, roasted tomatoes, smoked paprika, vinegar and salt, and pulse. With the machine running, drizzle in the 3 tbsp olive oil.
      - Stop while it still has texture, like a coarse pesto rather than a purée. Taste, then add salt, a splash of vinegar if it tastes heavy, or a sliver of habanero for a slow burn. Leave it at room temperature so the flavors come together.
  - section: Salmon, sous-vide path (T–50 → T–0)
    items:
      - Rinse off the brine quickly and pat the fillets *thoroughly* dry. Bag each one with 1 tbsp olive oil, a strip of lemon zest and a thyme sprig.
      - Seal on a gentle or moist setting, stopping as soon as the bag hugs the fish, because full suction crushes salmon. A zip-top bag with the water-displacement method works too.
      - Cook in the bath for 40–45 minutes for a 1-inch fillet straight from the fridge, or 50–55 minutes for 1½ inches. You then have about a 20-minute window with no change in texture, which is your timing buffer.
  - section: Salmon, pan-seared option (T–12 → T–0)
    items:
      - Skip the bath, the bag and the crackling. After the dry brine, rinse the skin-on fillets quickly and pat them *bone dry*, especially the skin. Let them sit out about 10 minutes while the sprouts start.
      - Heat a second heavy skillet (stainless, carbon steel or another cast iron) over medium-high with 1 tbsp of the olive oil until it shimmers. Lay the fillets in skin-side down, *away from you*, and press each one flat with a fish spatula for the first 20 seconds so the skin doesn't buckle.
      - Leave them alone for 4–5 minutes, lowering the heat to medium if it spits hard, until the skin is crisp and the flesh is opaque about two-thirds of the way up the sides.
      - Flip, add the thyme sprigs and lemon zest strips to the pan, and baste for 30–60 seconds, just until the center reads 120–125°F. Move the fillets skin-side up to a rack so the skin stays crisp.
  - section: Blackened sprouts (T–30 → T–5)
    items:
      - Pull the sprouts off the stalk, trim the woody bases, and peel off tired outer leaves. **Save the loose healthy leaves.** Halve each sprout through the core, leaving tiny ones whole and quartering any bigger than a walnut. Dry them completely, then slice the garlic and serrano and pick the thyme leaves.
      - Preheat the cast iron over medium-high for a full 5 minutes. Add the avocado oil and lay the sprouts cut-side down in one layer, not touching. Salt them and leave them alone for 4–5 minutes, until the cut faces are deep mahogany to black at the edges. Toss and cook another 2–3 minutes, until a knife slides into a core with slight resistance.
      - Turn the heat to medium. Add the butter, garlic, serrano and reserved leaves, and toss 60–90 seconds, until the butter foams nutty brown and the leaves crisp like chips. Take the pan off the heat, squeeze in the lemon, and scatter in thyme leaves.
  - section: Plate (T–0)
    items:
      - On warm plates, put a big spoonful of romesco off-center and drag the back of the spoon through it in one confident swoosh.
      - Slide a fish spatula under each fillet (bath salmon is fragile, so blot its underside on a paper towel). Set it on the romesco, brush the top with a little olive oil, and add flaky salt. Pan-seared fillets go on skin-side up.
      - Pile the sprouts alongside with the crispy leaves on top. For the sous-vide path, stand 1–2 shards of crackling in each fillet at an angle. Finish with a drizzle of olive oil over the romesco and a few thyme leaves.
tips:
  - The dry brine is what keeps sous-vide salmon from going mushy. It seasons the fish all the way through and firms the outer proteins so they don't push out white albumin. Use it for the pan-seared route too, because it also dries the skin for a better sear.
  - Frozen tomatoes are great for romesco. Freezing ruptures the cells, so the skins slip off in warm water, but it also releases a lot of water. Drain them and roast them down until jammy, or the sauce turns thin and pale.
  - Use lemon *zest* in the bag, not slices or juice. Over 45 minutes, sealed-in acid cures the surface and turns it chalky.
  - Don't sear the bath-cooked salmon, because a hard sear overcooks the outer layer and undoes the bath. If you want a crisp-skinned fillet, take the pan-seared route and cook it the whole way in the pan.
extras:
  - label: From the garden
    note: Frozen garden cherry tomatoes go into the romesco, garden thyme into the bag and over the sprouts, serrano into the butter, and a sliver of habanero into the romesco if you want a slow burn.
  - label: Make ahead
    note: Double the romesco (2 peppers, 1 cup tomatoes, ⅓ cup almonds, 1 oz bread, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp vinegar, 1 tsp pimentón, ⅓ cup oil) for a full-size food processor and a jar of leftovers. It keeps 5 days in the fridge and is better on day two. If you make it hours ahead, roast the garlic with the bread, because raw garlic gets sharper as it sits in acid. The crackling keeps a day in an airtight tin.
  - label: Food safety
    note: 122°F doesn't pasteurize the fish, so treat it like rare or sushi-style salmon. Buy the freshest you can and keep the total bath time under about 2 hours.
  - label: Gluten-free
    note: Swap the sourdough for an extra 2 tbsp of almonds. The sauce will be a little looser, so pulse in a few more almonds if it needs body.
  - label: Leftover romesco
    note: Spoon it over eggs, grilled chicken or charred eggplant, or use it as a dip for crudités.
  - label: If something goes sideways
    note: "Crackling still chewy? Give it 5 more minutes under the weight, since it needs longer, not hotter. Sprouts browning before the centers soften? Add a tablespoon of water and cover for 60 seconds, then uncover to dry the pan."

Two ways to cook the salmon. Use the bath for the silkiest fish, with crackling for crunch.
On a weeknight, pan-sear the fillets skin-on, and the crisp skin does the same job in one pan.
Either way, the smoky romesco made from garden tomatoes ties the plate together.
