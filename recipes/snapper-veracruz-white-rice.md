title: Snapper Veracruz over White Rice

pitch: >-
  This is the Gulf coast's answer to snapper — a saucy, savory braise that has
  nothing to do with sweet salsa. You build a quick tomato sauce loaded with
  sliced green olives, capers, and pickled jalapeño, then warm it with a whisper
  of cinnamon and clove until it tastes like the Yucatán. The snapper fillets go
  in skin-side up and poach right in the sauce, spooned over until they flake.
  *The briny-salty-tangy sauce does all the work — the fish just soaks it up and
  turns tender.* Serve it over white rice to catch every drop.

ingredients:
  - section: Veracruz sauce
    items:
      - 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
      - ½ white onion, thinly sliced
      - 3 cloves garlic, minced
      - 1 can (14 oz) canned tomatoes, with juices
      - ⅓ cup green olives, sliced
      - 2 tbsp capers, drained
      - 2 tbsp pickled jalapeños, sliced
      - 1 bay leaf
      - ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
      - 1 pinch ground cloves
      - ½ tsp dried oregano
  - section: Fish & finish
    items:
      - 2 red snapper fillets (about 6 oz each)
      - Salt and pepper
      - Juice of ½ lime
      - 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped
  - section: To serve
    items:
      - ¾ cup white rice, cooked per package
steps:
  - section: Rice
    items:
      - Start the rice first so it's ready when the fish is — cook ¾ cup white rice per the package directions and keep it covered.
  - section: Build the sauce
    items:
      - Warm the olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook until soft and translucent, 5–6 minutes. Stir in the garlic for 30 seconds.
      - Add the tomatoes (crush them with a spoon if whole), olives, capers, pickled jalapeños, bay leaf, cinnamon, cloves, and oregano. Simmer 10 minutes until the sauce thickens slightly.
  - section: Braise the fish
    items:
      - Season the snapper with salt and pepper and nestle the fillets into the sauce. Spoon sauce over the top, cover, and simmer gently until the fish flakes easily, 8–10 minutes.
      - Discard the bay leaf. Finish with lime juice and cilantro. Serve the fillets and plenty of sauce over the rice.
tips:
  - Green olives, capers, and pickled jalapeños are all salty — taste the sauce before adding any extra salt; you likely won't need it.
  - Keep the heat gentle once the fish is in — a hard boil breaks delicate fillets apart. A bare simmer keeps them intact.
  - The sauce is even better made ahead; simmer it a day early and reheat, then poach the fish just before serving.
extras:
  - label: Swap the fish
    note: "Veracruz sauce carries any firm-to-flaky white fish. *Cod, halibut, mahi-mahi, or grouper* are traditional — thicker fillets just need a couple more minutes under the lid. *Tilapia, sole, or flounder* are delicate: add them in the last 5 minutes and don't stir, or they'll fall apart. *Sea bass* is excellent. Avoid oily fish like salmon here — it fights the bright, briny sauce. Whatever you use, keep the sauce identical; only the poaching time changes with thickness."
  - label: From the garden
    note: Swap the pickled jalapeño for a fresh garden jalapeño or serrano (add it with the onion) for a greener, sharper heat, and finish with garden cilantro.
  - label: Make it a plate
    note: A side of black beans or warm corn tortillas turns this into a full spread. Leftover sauce is great over eggs the next morning.

*Pescado a la veracruzana* is the classic — Spanish olives and capers meeting
New World tomato and chile. It's proof that "fish night" can be deeply savory
and saucy, not just a fillet with a squeeze of lemon.
