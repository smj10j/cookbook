ingredients:
  - section: Orzo bowls
    items:
      - 5 oz orzo
      - ½ lb broccoli, cut into small florets (stems peeled and sliced)
      - 1 oz sun-dried tomato strips (not oil-packed), sliced
      - 1 red onion, halved and thinly sliced
      - 2 tbsp pine nuts
      - 3 tbsp crumbled goat cheese
      - 1 tbsp olive oil
      - 1 tsp red chile flakes (optional)
      - Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  - section: Basil pistou
    items:
      - 1½ cups packed fresh basil leaves
      - 2 cloves garlic
      - ¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil
      - 2 tbsp finely grated Parmesan
      - 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
      - ½ tsp kosher salt
steps:
  - section: Prep
    items:
      - Bring a medium pot of well-salted water to a boil. Add the orzo and cook until al dente, about 8–9 minutes. Scoop out ½ cup of the pasta cooking water, then drain the orzo and toss with a drizzle of olive oil so it doesn't clump.
      - Toast the pine nuts in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, until golden and fragrant, 2–3 minutes. Tip onto a plate right away so they don't scorch.
  - section: Make the pistou
    items:
      - In a food processor (or a mortar and pestle), pulse the garlic, lemon juice, and salt together and let sit 5 minutes to mellow the garlic's raw edge.
      - Add the basil, Parmesan, and olive oil and pulse just until the basil is finely chopped but not fully pureed — you want a loose, flecked sauce, not a smooth paste. Taste and adjust salt and lemon.
  - section: Build the bowls
    items:
      - Heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a large frying pan over medium-high. Add the broccoli and cook, stirring occasionally, until crisp-tender and lightly charred in spots, 3–5 minutes.
      - Add the onion, sun-dried tomatoes, and as much chile flakes as you like, and cook until the onion starts to soften, 2–3 minutes.
      - Stir in the drained orzo and a splash of the reserved pasta water and cook until heated through and glossy, 1–2 minutes, adding more pasta water if it looks dry.
      - Remove from the heat and stir in the pistou to taste (start with about half, add more to your liking). Season with salt and pepper.
      - Divide between two bowls and garnish with the toasted pine nuts and crumbled goat cheese.
tips:
  - Add the pistou off the heat — cooking fresh basil sauce dulls its color and flavor. Any leftover keeps a few days in the fridge under a thin film of oil; it's great on eggs, fish, or toast.
  - If your sun-dried tomatoes are leathery-dry, soak them in a little warm water (or the hot pasta water) for 5 minutes to soften before slicing.
  - Reserve more pasta water than you think you need — the starchy liquid is what makes the orzo saucy instead of sticky as it reheats in the pan.
  - Goat cheese melts into a creamy sauce if you stir it in warm, or stays in cool tangy pockets if you scatter it on at the end — both are good, so choose your texture.
extras:

    note: This is the recipe for a big basil harvest — make a double batch of pistou and freeze half in an ice-cube tray. A little garden garlic and lemon thyme are welcome in the sauce too.
  - label: Make it a meal
    note: Fold in a can of drained white beans or chickpeas with the orzo for more protein, or top with a jammy soft-boiled egg. Grilled or pan-seared salmon or chicken also sits beautifully on top.

Sunbasket's original arrives with the pistou pre-made in a cup; this version builds
it from scratch the classic Provençal way — basil, garlic, lemon, and good olive oil
pounded loose — with a spoonful of Parmesan stirred in for savory depth. Stirred in
raw at the very end, it keeps the whole warm bowl tasting like summer.
